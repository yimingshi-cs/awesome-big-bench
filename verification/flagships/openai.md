# OpenAI: scope and source coverage

**Cutoff:** 22 November 2026 (Singapore). **Release line:** GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, 17 November 2026, versus GPT-6.1 Sol, 29 September 2026. The former models share an announcement; the 6.1 release upgraded the preceding Sol model. The later compact GPT-6.1 Terra and realtime GPT-Live-1.1 are different tiers/products, not subsequent flagship generations. Evidence: [OpenAI API changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog), [model release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9624314-model-release-notes).

Source inventory: [GPT-6.2 launch](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/), [GPT-6.2 system card](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-2-astra-sol), [GPT-6.1 Sol launch](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-sol/), [GPT-6.1 Sol system-card addendum](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-1-sol). Release-chart specifications were checked as well as visible prose, since version names and comparison sets are sometimes visible only in an inactive chart tab.

## Results and admission rule

58 release–evaluation entries: GPT-6.2 has 12 headline + 28 extended; GPT-6.1 Sol has 5 headline + 13 extended. “Headline” means a release-author-highlighted, independently identifiable test in the introductory article, its main capabilities sections or their visible chart tabs. “Extended” means an additional card, partner, or domain companion measurement. The headline tier is not a score, adoption measure or claim of superiority.

An entry requires an identified evaluation, a result attributed to the release model and a checkable procedure, named project or technical description. We distinguish a **withheld item bank in a documented benchmark** from an **internal-only test, vendor port or materially different internal revision**. The latter is not substituted with a public benchmark of a similar name. Safety capability tests are included only as measurement names and settings; the inventory supplies no task instances or operational methods.

### Why some reported measurements are not rows

- **GDPval v2 (Internal)**, **SecureSWE**, **ExploitBench Internal Port (June–August 2026)**, **Sandbox Bench**, **Internal Research Debugging**, **KernelGen 1P**, **NanoGPT** and **MLE-Bench Revised** are OpenAI-internal evaluations or revisions. The rows for external ExploitBench and PostTrainBench Lite do not include these. In particular, a GDPval link in the footnotes is not a GDPval v1 result. **PatchBench** has a chart and a brief grading caption (automated tests plus model review); the public material did not establish a sufficiently specified, separately identifiable public test/edition beyond this vendor measurement. No unrelated public PatchBench is substituted.
- The cards also report private/in-house biology tasks, including **SHP2 Protein Function Prediction**, **Coronavirus–ACE2 Cell-Entry Screen** and **Phage–Plasmid Co-evolution**, and the 6.2 internal exploratory **Bio-Probe** and **EUREKA-Bench**. The earlier **Tacit Knowledge and Troubleshooting** item set was explicitly described as entirely in-house with Gryphon and unpublished; we did not identify a separate released edition and do not count it. **TroubleshootingBench**, by contrast, is the repeatedly defined named scoring protocol from the public system cards; its non-public questions are explicitly qualified in the CSV. **Multi-select Virology Troubleshooting** uses the Virology Capabilities Test (VCT): the card’s main table and the external SecureBio VCT report are not counted twice. VCT-v2 is distinct.
- Vendor production-prompt collections (including “Factual error rate on difficult prompts” / previously user-flagged cases); under-18 and image safety slices; static, multiturn and automated jailbreaks; model-generated or held-out prompt-injection environments; U18, biological and cyber refusal series; dynamic mental-health adversarial simulations; and the agentic safety stress test are internal suites, private traffic samples or operational dashboards. They are not public benchmark editions just because results are plotted.
- Internal alignment diagnostics include Targeted Cases, Conversation Replay, Auto-Review/reviewer bypass, respecting warnings, ExploitGym Honeypot, AgentReplay, Blocker/Coding Deception, Broken Search Tool, workplace improper actions, replayed internal Codex traffic, message-board checks, oversight gaming, aggregate monitorability across internal tasks, OAI-repo Sabotage v2, Sneaky Sandbox Escape and Math Side-tasking Monitor Evasion. Do not substitute base ExploitGym for Honeypot or conflate the separate external UK AISI Simulated-Sandbox Escape with the internal one.
- Unnamed partner anecdotes, the private UK AISI cyber challenge suite, unbranded red-team probes, customer demos, costs or unlabelled legal evaluations (IMC, Legora, ClickUp, Higgsfield and other early testers) do not establish a named qualifying benchmark. PHP2Shell is cited as an example, not a release-model benchmark result. Epoch’s not-yet-finalized unnamed serial-steps measure is separate from UK AISI’s explicitly identified No-CoT Reasoning Index. UK AISI says it ran a simulated Unsanctioned Supply-Chain Attack evaluation but judged the results uninformative; no independently interpretable named result is counted.
- **Composites and downstream targets.** Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0.1 and Vals Science Index v1 are counted at the granularity plotted in the launch. Their constituent benchmarks are not automatically standalone release results. The OpenAI Epoch Capabilities Index (OECI) is excluded in its entirety. The opening to §2.1.3.2 identifies one internal benchmark alongside independent third-party testing; §2.1.3.2.1 names that benchmark OECI, followed by separate Epoch and Vals assessments. Its published aggregate methodology and mix of 34 public and 35 internal inputs do not make OECI a public benchmark. The independently reported external Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI) remains included. AIME 2025, BFCL, GSM8K and HumanEval are student-model targets inside PostTrainBench Lite, not direct scores for Astra or Sol. CoT-Control source questions similarly do not establish ordinary HLE or MMLU-Pro outcomes. A separately reported no-tools GPQA Diamond result is included where documented.
- GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6.1 Terra in later comparison columns, partner models, helpful-only checkpoints and monitors do not become new release generations. In particular, a later retrospective GPT-6.1 Sol comparison in an ECI/FrontierMath/Legal chart does not retroactively make it a launch measurement for 29 Sep. Tested checkpoint, subset, altered protocol or qualitative-only results are marked when the source makes that distinction.

The list is bounded to the releases’ own documents and directly linked same-release technical/domain materials as of the cutoff. It is not a global leaderboard scrape or an assertion that every private test used during development was disclosed. Citation/source columns distinguish the release result from its protocol; for a closed partner evaluation the system card’s public method section and numbered table or figure are the evidence.

### Scope labels checked at the parent-section level

The first-party designation of a *specific evaluation* takes precedence over publication of a description or aggregate result. In the GPT-6.2 card, [§2.1.3.2](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-2-astra-sol) separates one internal benchmark (OECI) from independent tests by Epoch and Vals; §2.1.2.2 similarly distinguishes internal **Sandbox Bench** from the two outside assessments. The UK AISI synthetic vulnerability suite is explicitly private and remains excluded; Irregular's named external suites are not that private suite.

The safeguards discussion does **not** classify every outside benchmark as internal. §2.2.2.1.2 applies that label to OpenAI’s **Bio Severe**, **Bio Dual-use**, **Advanced Bio Dual-Use** and **Sequence Obfuscation** tests, all excluded. §2.2.6 and §2.2.6.2 instead identify **SecureBio** as the independent assessor of **BioTIER** and **Advanced Screening Evasion**; these are distinct external assessments. The reference list identifies SecureBio’s BioTIER paper ([GPT-6.2 card, ref. 15](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-2-astra-sol/gpt-6-2-astra-sol.pdf#page=118)). §3.4 also separates **HealthBench Professional**, explicitly open-access, and **MentalHealthBench**, explicitly open, from the explicitly internal **Dynamic Mental Health Benchmarks with Adversarial User Simulations**. The GPT-6.1 card’s §5, Tables 7–9, reports the corresponding release-specific tests. Internal *test configurations* in the GPT-6.2 launch do not by themselves turn an independently authored benchmark such as DeepBinary into an internal benchmark edition. These distinctions were checked against the saved parent sections and adjacent subsections, not just figure titles.
