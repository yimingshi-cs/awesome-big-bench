# Claude release coverage · public evidence through 22 November 2026 (SGT)

The two successive top-capability, general-purpose Claude generations were **Fable/Mythos 5.2** (9 November 2026) and **Fable/Mythos 5.1** (1 September 2026). Fable and Mythos are the same underlying model with different safeguards and access; they are one release per generation, not two independent model generations. The launch calls Opus 5.5 its strongest *daily driver* and directs the most demanding work to Fable/Mythos. The later Sonnet and Haiku 5.5 announcements concern different performance/cost tiers. Neither the numeric suffix nor an Opus, GPT or Gemini column in an evaluation table determines which release is the previous flagship.

**Date reconciliation:** Anthropic’s [Platform release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview#september-1-2026) explicitly announce both 5.1 configurations on 1 September. Its [Fable 5.1 model page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/overview) and 5.1 system-card cover agree. The [news index](https://www.anthropic.com/news/models) labels the 5.1 announcement listing 15 September; we use the earlier evidenced availability, not the listing date. The November date appears on the 5.2 launch page and card.

## What the comparison measures

| Scope | 5.2: configurations / families | 5.1: configurations / families | Families in both |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Main launch comparison table only | 15 / 14 | 9 / 7 | 4 |
| Launches, attributed named partner results and system-card assessments | 104 / 90 | 79 / 66 | 57 |

The four families that occur **in both main launch tables** are **AutomationBench, Humanity’s Last Exam, OSWorld and Terminal-Bench-Science**. OSWorld is 2.0 in the 5.1 launch and the 2.1 offline subset in the 5.2 launch. Sharing a family does not mean identical tasks, scaffolds, graders, or eligibility for a score comparison. Likewise, leaving the headline table does not mean a test was dropped: CursorBench moved from the 5.1 lead table to the full 5.2 system card, and the version changed.

Each CSV row is one observed benchmark/configuration, not a new benchmark. Family counts deduplicate variants, subsets, graders and harnesses within a generation. The explicit family column is used without fuzzy matching: ARC-AGI-1/2/3 roll up to ARC-AGI, all SWE-bench variants to SWE-bench, and HealthBench Professional to HealthBench. Terminal-Bench-Science stays a named science suite distinct from the terminal-work suite. Anthropic’s formerly named OSS-Fuzz evaluation and its renamed Binary Exploitation Benchmark are both excluded: these are internal Anthropic variants, not the upstream project. A row on a public dataset used for a *different* target (for example reasoning-trace controllability on GPQA, HLE and MMLU-Pro) records that use explicitly and does not imply a standard QA leaderboard result.

**The full-family count is not a count of public downloadable benchmarks.** Extended includes external academic and commercial evaluations, publicly described proprietary protocols, named partner or independent-assessor tests, and formal Anthropic research assessments (including alignment and model welfare). Those last assessments answer questions different from coding and reasoning capability. For a public post about what labs *highlight*, use the first line of the table. For a research directory, use the rows and protocol notes; the second line is not a list of widely adopted independent benchmarks. The family union after screening is 99 (17 headline).

## Documents and classification

- [5.2 release](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) and [5.2 system card, 264 PDF pages](https://assets.claude.com/27d60918d218125e7504bc7354c4b4626ec56b11.pdf).
- [5.1 release](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1) and [5.1 system card, 212 PDF pages](https://assets.claude.com/0339e6a7c5c7b87f5c07798616dc32c215d14235.pdf).
- The [publisher’s card index](https://www.anthropic.com/system-cards) identifies one joint card per pair. No separate third flagship technical report is represented by a comparator card. We reviewed the launches’ main tables, table notes, surrounding examples, partner quotations and scientific sections; the full joint cards, including the text, table/figure captions, numbered methods and appendices; and named linked protocols where needed.
- **Headline:** the comparison table on that generation’s own launch page, not Table 8.1.A in its PDF. **Extended:** named public tests or publicly described assessments outside that table. Partner-only named results are explicitly attributed (Crosby’s RedlineBench and Samaya’s FrontierFinance on 5.1; Snowflake’s data-eng-bench on 5.2), not presented as Anthropic reruns. In the CSV, the release URL identifies the generation; the protocol URL points to the relevant card page for vendor recipes, or to a primary project/owner for off-card benchmarks. The numbered section is given separately.
- Both cards: §1 model identity and configuration; §2 RSP assessments and external METR testing; §3 cybersecurity and safeguards; §4 harmlessness, bias and health; §5 agentic safety; §6 alignment, honesty and oversight; §7 model welfare; §8 capabilities and configuration-level results; §9 welfare interview questions and HLE blocklist. In particular, named tests outside §8 such as BBQ, AA-Omniscience, MASK, SHADE-Arena, VCT and METR’s Budget NanoGPT Speedrun were not inferred from the launch table.
- Distinctions preserved include FrontierCode 1.1 Main/Extended versus the rebuilt 2.0; BenchCAD’s original and 2.1 Core tasks; ProgramBench’s reference-filtered tasks; single- vs multi-agent DRACO; with/without tools; public OfficeQA versus the OfficeQA Pro subset, and Harvey’s private LAB evaluation; launch vs external or prerelease checks; and the two ARC-AGI-3 harnesses. Published post-release software may since have changed: use the dated method, not today’s leaderboard default.
- **Internal is a release-specific exclusion, not a price, license or authorship label.** Exclude a benchmark or reported variant identified as an internal evaluation, internal fork, internal replication, or privately operated organizational diagnostic. Publishing a description/score of that internal variant does not make it eligible. A private item bank held by another benchmark owner, a public benchmark run in the lab, an otherwise publicly defined method authored by the lab, or use of nonpublic model weights is *not*, by itself, an exclusion. If the original benchmark has since been released, check that the version actually tested is the same public protocol; do not automatically promote an internal fork or discard a now-published benchmark because of its origins.
- Examples removed: STEM Professor Bench; Real-World Finance v2 (expressly internally developed); CoBench; CritPT-Corrected; the Anthropic ECI (a fork powered by internal results, not Epoch’s public ECI); the renamed Anthropic OSS-Fuzz version; rewind-attacker; inspection of private RL/deployment episodes; internal replicas of externally published reasoning-trace experiments; and the particular 5.1 Chartography, BenchCAD, GDP.pdf, Toolathlon and LAB internal implementations. The independent Artificial Analysis held-out LAB row stays; it is not the internal Anthropic reimplementation.
- Examples retained: the standard RoboDojo simulator run by Anthropic; both public Terminal-Bench releases run on Anthropic’s installation with Claude Code; Irregular’s CyScenarioBench with third-party held-out tasks; Zapier’s AutomationBench; the official BenchCAD 2.1 scorer; 5.2 original Chartography tasks scored using the leaderboard grader (the separate regenerated-chart check is excluded); [ExploitBench](https://exploitbench.ai/) and the public Berkeley-led ExploitGym; Anthropic’s open-source political even-handedness assessment; [Anthropic’s BioMysteryBench release](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/BioMysteryBench-full). In 5.2, WANDR is identified as Anthropic’s **published, modified methodology** (different tool/judge recipe); it is not the original Perplexity result. The card does not describe this as an internal-only version. A catalog may label it “modified”; it must not silently equate the two.
- **Safety and welfare were checked at the protocol level.** Anthropic’s private-session-seeded automated audit and the manual interviews requiring internal development files were removed. The separate model-circumstances protocol is accompanied by seed questions in each card’s appendix; publicly described task-preference, welfare tradeoff and constitution experiments are not labelled internal versions. The single-turn harmful/benign request protocol remains, but Anthropic’s in-house multi-turn case construction is explicitly out of the retained row. External safety tests are not automatically accepted: the UK AISI supply-chain result used an explicitly internal Petri version and is excluded. Conversely, separate UK AISI measurements of trace controllability in the 5.2 card have their own provenance and remain; they do not restore Anthropic’s excluded internal replicas. No private tasks or operational instructions are included.
- The earlier comparison counted **117/98 configuration rows and 103/81 families** for 5.2/5.1 (67 shared). After screening: **104/79 rows, 90/66 families, 57 shared**. The main-table family counts are unchanged. The exclusion ledger identifies whole rows versus components and already-excluded mentions, so its length is not the number to subtract. The unenumerated components of AECI are not inferred, 5.2 CoBench was not reported, and the 5.1 card explicitly lacked an ARC-AGI-3 result. Later comparisons are not backfilled into an earlier launch inventory.
- Chapter 8 of the 5.1 card states that many life-sciences tasks were internally developed, but does not assign that description to every individual task. We do not turn that group-level statement into an item-level allegation. Third-party protocols (e.g., LatchBio and Benchling), published benchmarks with a defined subset (e.g., ProteinGym), and card-defined tasks for which no item-level internal designation was found are differentiated in the row notes. For the latter, public method descriptions are evidence of what was run, not proof that their grading sets are downloadable.
- Some independent reports, pre-release checkpoints, private grader versions, safety-classifier configurations and unavailable test sets prevent exact replication. Unnamed internal finance/browser evaluations, a partner’s informal impressions, case studies such as hardware demonstrations, individual novel research problems and links in the HLE blocklist were not treated as standalone benchmark releases. External evaluations that are merely mentioned or whose outcomes were not available were not assumed to have scored a particular configuration. No scores, private tasks or operational safety-test details are reproduced.
