# Google DeepMind: Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon

**Cutoff: November 22, 2026.** The focal releases are **Gemini 4 Carbon** (first announced [October 28](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-carbon/); [broad availability November 10](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/)) and its direct frontier predecessor, **Gemini 4 Argon** ([October 13](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/)). Carbon builds on Argon and is positioned for harder, longer-horizon work. These are two successive general-purpose **flagship releases**, not the Gemini 4 and Gemini 3 major-version families. [Hydrogen, November 12](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/), and [Neon, November 18](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/), are faster execution/latency offerings within Gemini 4. The live [family page](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/) leads with these newer models; its different tables are **not** one combined benchmark list.

**89 release × protocol records:** Carbon 25 headline + 17 extended; Argon 5 headline + 42 extended. This is not 89 globally unique benchmarks. The asymmetry is partly editorial: Carbon's announcement embeds a full benchmark table, while Argon's full table appears in its model card rather than the announcement. A headline row is also often repeated in the card or report; it is counted once, at its highest tier for that release. Public, stable, scored tests defined in the report itself are included even when their task banks are not downloadable (e.g., Gram, Impossible Tasks Evaluation, and Personality Evaluation). A publication presenting both comparison models is assigned to the focal launch, not backdated to the earlier model. The CSV date is always the first **release date**; the location specifies later November availability evidence where applicable.

## Attribution checks that change the list

- **GraphWalks** is [OpenAI's public benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/graphwalks). Both releases use BFS F1. Argon reports the up-to-128k bucket and the 256k–1M bucket; Carbon reports only 256k–1M (200 problems). Do not silently turn either into the entire benchmark.
- **OSWorld**: Argon reports 2.0, offline subset, partial credit, 08.08 patch; Carbon reports 2.1, the 82-task offline subset. The October 28 methodology explicitly retested Argon for the newer comparison. That establishes a later 2.1 result, **not** a 2.1 benchmark in the October 13 Argon launch. [Versioned benchmark releases](https://github.com/xlang-ai/OSWorld-V2/releases).
- **Terminal-Bench**: both focal releases report 4.0; Argon computes its own run, Carbon cites Artificial Analysis. [4.0 is a specific task revision](https://www.tbench.ai/news/terminal-bench-4-0). Terminal-Bench Science 0.1 is a distinct task suite, not an alternate label for 4.0. 4.1 in the later Neon/Hydrogen materials is not a substitute.
- **AutomationBench**: both focal reports use the private set via [Zapier's public leaderboard](https://zapier.com/benchmarks). [AutomationBench-AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/announcing-zapier-automationbench-aa) is Artificial Analysis's separately administered evaluation over Zapier tasks. The [Hydrogen methodology](https://deepmind.google/models/evals-methodology/gemini-4-hydrogen/) documents AA provenance; that is not evidence of a stand-alone AutomationBench-AA claim for Carbon or Argon. Likewise, inclusion inside a composite is not a separate focal result.
- **CWE-bench:** Argon explicitly says v1; Carbon's unversioned headline is **v1** per its methodology and November release. The [project's v2](https://cwe-bench.com/) is independently public, distinct from [v1](https://cwe-bench.com/?v=v1). **SEC-bench Pro** is [separately identifiable](https://sec-bench.github.io/), and Carbon actually reports its May 2026 split. Do not backdate a comparator-column score into an Argon launch claim.
- **WebExploitBench v1.2** is named and methodologically specified by [Neon](https://deepmind.google/models/evals-methodology/gemini-4-neon/); [the benchmark operator](https://eval.agentcyberrange.io/) documents the evaluation. Do not infer that every scored task is freely downloadable. It was not located as a focal Carbon/Argon benchmark. The same distinction applies to the later version of CWE-bench. No exploit or reproduction instructions are part of this inventory.
- **Gamow RareBench and LabBench:** the Argon release calls the partner data private, but [RareBench](https://gamowlabs.com/rarebench-0-1) and [LabBench](https://gamowlabs.com/labbench-benchmarking-ai-wet-lab-decisions) define public tasks and metrics. The local data are not a different internal-only benchmark, so both qualify. Gamow LabBench is **not** [LABBench2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09554), which the Argon model evaluation reports separately.
- **Safety and factuality:** HealthBench Hard and HealthBench Professional, SimpleQA Verified, AA-Omniscience, FACTS Grounding and HelpBench are directly named public evaluations. Argon's HLE, GPQA and MMLU-Pro rows are **selected QA stimuli** in monitoring/control protocols, not general capability scores. Brew is split out because the report reuses it for a separate experimental condition, not merely because it belongs to NCRI. Carbon explicitly refreshes NCRI in its own report; its comparison tables are not blanket grounds to import every Argon protocol.
- **Report-defined assessments:** Gram simulates 17 deployment scenarios; this does not mean its task bank is open, or that it is the same as the internal-codebase honeypots. Impossible Tasks has tables of scenarios and loss categories; ambiguous/high-risk tasks have collaboration/counter-collaboration criteria. Personality Evaluation describes a prompt suite and scoring composites. Report-only protocols are linked to the relevant page and labelled for what they are; the report's categories or scalar safety rates do not all become independent benchmarks.

## Exclusions and unresolved evidence

* **Named internal versions excluded:** Google RE-Bench/GRB (not METR RE-Bench), GCI v1 (internal index, not the Epoch Capabilities Index), Wiz Penetration Testing Benchmark as described by Argon's methodology, internal vulnerability discovery/research, tool replication, Fortify, in-production/RL monitoring, proprietary factuality/search tests and internal prompt-injection suites. In particular, the internal coding-agent stress test *uses* SWE-Bench Pro tasks; it is not a reported SWE-Bench Pro score. Merely analogizing to XSTest or OR-Bench is not using either one.
* **Not separately counted:** single demonstrations, science case studies, regulator/agency engagement without a distinct publicly defined metric, isolated table metrics, one-off red-team anecdotes, and broad prohibited-content/FSF policy categories. A condition or worksheet from a scored protocol is not another benchmark unless the report independently identifies and applies it.
* **Public mention without a verified matching protocol:** Carbon's November launch names Hebbia's E2E Financial Agentic evaluation and Balyasny's General Financial Intelligence benchmark. [Hebbia's older Financial AI Benchmark](https://www.hebbia.com/blog/which-model-will-give-me-the-edge) is not enough to establish the identity of the particular E2E evaluation. For Balyasny, the launch lists domains and a ranking but not a protocol sufficient to verify the named benchmark. Neither is treated as an independently verified case. Add if the provider publishes a matching method or project.
* The model-specific method PDFs and safety reports are the linked technical records used here; no additional broad standalone Gemini 4 Argon/Carbon technical report was identified in the official source chain before the cutoff. This is an inventory of publicly identifiable protocols attributable to these release packets, not every internal test Google ran.
